My work examines how political and economic structures shape relationships among land, labor, mobility, and environmental change—and how communities, social movements, and public institutions contest and transform those arrangements. Across my research, teaching, and policy work, I focus on the U.S. and global political economy of agri-food systems, climate change and resilience, and migration and displacement, and their relationship to anticolonial and anticapitalist world-building from the twentieth century into the present.

I am Director of Research and Global Justice Senior Policy Analyst at the Othering & Belonging Institute at UC Berkeley, where I lead and develop interdisciplinary research agendas, projects, and collaborations connecting scholarship, policy, and community-based knowledge. My work is grounded in a commitment to applied research and public impact on questions of racial, economic, environmental, and climate justice, and has included research across the United States, Africa, Asia-Pacific, and Latin America and the Caribbean.

My applied research engages public agencies, policymakers, advocates, and community-based organizations on questions of climate displacement and resilience, land and food systems, migration, and just transitions. Much of this work focuses on translating interdisciplinary research into policy-relevant frameworks, public resources, and strategies responsive to the needs and knowledge of affected communities. This includes serving as a Coordinating Lead Author for California’s Fifth Climate Change Assessment and advising UNHCR on climate change, nationality, and statelessness.

My academic research has appeared in Environment and Planning D: Society and Space, Comparative American Studies, and Qui Parle, with chapters in volumes on industrial education and development, comparative ethnic studies, and land and belonging. I hold a Ph.D. in Society and Environment from the Department of Environmental Science, Policy, and Management at UC Berkeley and have held academic appointments at Tufts University, Williams College, San José State University, and Santa Clara University.
