Teaching

In my teaching, I engage students in critical analyses of the politics of land, agriculture, the environment, and climate, with particular attention to their intersections with power, wealth, labor, race, and gender. I situate these dynamics within longer histories of social and environmental struggle and in relation to contemporary movements for justice and transformation.

I design courses that connect these broader structures and histories to students’ own lives, communities, and aspirations. Through collaborative and reflective learning practices, I invite students to draw on personal, family, and community knowledge while developing the analytical tools to question inherited assumptions, understand competing perspectives, and imagine alternative social and environmental futures.
